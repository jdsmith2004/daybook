After a slip

Does a relapse reset all my progress?

No. The days you went without it really happened, and they are still yours. Getting better is not a straight line, and a setback can be part of it without undoing it.

Why it feels like a reset

Many people picture quitting as a streak that grows until it is done. When a counter drops to zero, it feels like every good day was wasted. That zero is a way of counting, not a fact about you. Think of where you started: a slip after weeks of good days is a long way from where you began, and that distance is real progress.

What getting better really looks like

A US health agency, SAMHSA, describes recovery as “non-linear, characterized by continual growth and improved functioning that may involve setbacks.” Getting better shows up across a whole life: your health, a steady home, things that matter to you, and people around you. A streak is one part of that, not the whole picture.

Habit research points the same way. In a study where 96 volunteers each built a new daily habit, missing a single day made little difference to how automatic the habit became. What added up was a string of misses. So the day after a slip is the day that matters most, and it is a day you can win.

Count what you kept

Writer James Clear puts it this way: every action is a vote for the type of person you wish to become. Each urge you rode out was a vote. A slip is one vote, not the whole count. These are all real progress:

  • An honest log, even after a slip.
  • An urge ridden out.
  • A night with the phone outside the bedroom.
  • A text to a real person.

One thing to do tonight

Write down how many good days you had before this slip, and one thing that helped you get them. That is your evidence, and you can use it again tomorrow.

How Daybook helps

Daybook never counts down to zero. It counts the no-slip days in your recent weeks, so one hard day stays one hard day. Points, levels and badges only go up, and a slip never takes them away. The flame on Today keeps every bit of strength it has earned.
