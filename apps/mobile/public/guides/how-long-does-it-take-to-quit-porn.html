Bigger questions

How long does it take to quit porn?

There is no single number that fits everyone, and that is honest, not discouraging. What research does show: new habits take weeks to become automatic, often around two months; the path is not a straight line; and single misses do not undo it.

New habits take weeks, not days

Quitting is mostly building new habits in place of the old one: the phone charging in the kitchen, a walk at the hard hour, a text instead of a scroll. In a study where 96 volunteers each built one small daily habit over 12 weeks, habits took weeks to become automatic, often around two months. Missing a single day made little difference. What added up was a string of misses.

Not a straight line

A US health agency, SAMHSA, describes recovery as “non-linear, characterized by continual growth and improved functioning that may involve setbacks.” Good weeks and hard days can both be part of getting better. The direction matters more than any single day.

Who you are becoming

The change that lasts is a change in who you are, not only in what you do. Researchers followed 132 adults through a treatment community for alcohol and drugs. Over the first month, most came to see themselves less as users and more as people in recovery, and the bigger that shift, the better they did later. You can start that change now, long before any finish line.

Signs you are getting better

  • Urges you ride out, even strong ones.
  • Better sleep, and the phone outside the bedroom.
  • An honest log, even after a slip.
  • More time with real people and things that matter to you.

One thing to do tonight

Write one sentence about who you are becoming, like “I am someone who goes to bed with the phone in the kitchen.” Then do one thing tonight that person would do.
