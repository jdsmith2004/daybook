In the hard minute

Why do I relapse at night?

Same person, same goal, same phone. What changes at night is your state: more tired, often more alone, in the same room at the same hour as before. Each of those makes an urge look bigger than it is, and each one is something you can work with.

Tired makes the reward look bigger

In a lab study, volunteers who were kept up all night had a stronger reward response in the brain to pictures, and rated more of them as pleasant, than people who had slept. A worn-out brain reacts more strongly to anything that feels good. That is a state you are in, not who you are.

Lonely makes comfort feel urgent

In a survey of 1,247 adults, loneliness and porn use rose together. The researchers think it may run both ways: feel lonely, reach for porn, feel more cut off, feel lonelier. Late at night, a lonely mind wants comfort, and porn promises it fast. A real person, even by text, meets that need better.

The bed and the phone are a cue

Habits lean on their setting: the room, the hour, the device. When students moved to a new university and their surroundings changed a lot, their old habits broke, and what they did followed their own intentions more. For many people the setting of a slip is the same every time: in bed, late, phone in hand. Change that setting, and the cue gets weaker.

It also helps to decide before the night comes. An if-then plan moves the choice to a calm moment: “If I am in bed and reach for my phone, then I get up and get a glass of water.” A large review of many studies found that people who made if-then plans followed through on their goals more than people who only set the goal.

One thing to do tonight

Charge your phone outside the bedroom tonight. A cheap alarm clock solves the alarm problem. If you feel lonely, send one text to a real person first. It does not have to be about this.
