Privacy policy

In effect from October 24, 2026. Apple Health, and What Daybook learns, added November 9, 2026.

The short version

  • Without an account, your log never leaves your phone.
  • With an account, your records are kept under your account and sent only to you, when you are signed in.
  • No ads, and nothing is ever sold or shared. What the app counts about its own use is below, and it is never tied to your name.
  • Daybook learns how the app is used, to make it better: what you open, finish and choose, and how many urges and slips you log. It is anonymous: never tied to your name, email or account, and never your journal, your notes or anything you write. It is never sold or shared, and you can turn it off.
  • If you connect Apple Health, Daybook reads only how long you slept, and it stays on your phone. It is never sent, even with an account.
  • If you leave a tip, Apple or Stripe handles the payment. Daybook keeps only a small supporter record, never your card details.
  • You can delete everything, and your account, from inside the app.

Who runs Daybook

Daybook is made by Jeff Smith, an independent developer. Questions about this policy or your information go to jeffreydsmith.cs@gmail.com.

Without an account

Everything you put in Daybook (your log of urges and slips, check-ins, notes, your journal, your plan, lessons, reviews and settings) is stored on your device only, or in your browser's storage when you use Daybook on the web. Daybook does not receive any of it. (The anonymous counts below, feedback you choose to send, and a tip you choose to leave, are not part of your log.)

With an account

An account is optional. When you make one, Daybook keeps:

  • Your email address and password, to sign you in and, once you have confirmed it, to send you a code if you forget your password. The password is handled by Amazon Cognito and stored in a form that cannot be read back.
  • If you sign in with Apple: Apple's ID for you (a random code Apple makes for Daybook) and the email Apple gives (your own, or a Hide My Email address if you chose that), kept on your account in place of a password. Daybook checks Apple's sign-in each time and keeps nothing else from Apple. When you delete the account, Daybook asks Apple once more and tells Apple to end Daybook's sign-in.
  • Your records, so they are safe if you lose your phone and the same on every device: your log (times, what set things off, what helped, notes, the feelings you name, including before and after a slip), the plans you make in Talk it through and after a slip, check-ins (how you felt, how strong urges were, and how you slept), your answer to “How are you feeling today?”, your journal (each entry's feeling, what you wrote, the question it answers, and when), your plan and reasons, setup answers (which can include how much faith matters to you, and which faith), Sunday reviews, lesson progress (which lessons you finished, and when), and settings.
  • An account ID, a random number that your records are stored under.
  • Your supporter record, if you tipped or had an account before tips opened sync (see Tips).

Your records are used for one thing: sending them back to you, on your devices, when you are signed in. They are not viewed, analyzed, sold, shared, used for ads, or used to train anything.

What Daybook never collects

  • Your location, contacts, photos, browsing, or any advertising identifier.
  • Tracking across other apps or websites, and no ad or tracking code from anyone else. What Daybook learns (below) stays with Daybook, is never tied to who you are, and is used only to make the app better.
  • Voice notes, and the notes you leave for the next hard moment. They stay on your phone, with or without an account: not synced, not in the safe copy, never sent.
  • Anything about your Home Screen. The flame widget is drawn on your phone from your own records, and shows no count and nothing that says what Daybook is for. To give its points once, the app asks your phone only whether Daybook's own widget is on a screen, and keeps the day it first was.
  • The words of your reminders, or when they came. Daybook learns only which kind arrived, was opened, or was followed by what it was for (below). Daybook's reminders are made on your phone, and which wordings help, and when, stays on your phone and is never sent. To time them, the phone keeps the times the app was opened for 28 days; those times stay on your phone too. The lesson reminder comes at about the time you finished your last lesson, read from your own records on the phone.
  • Anything from Screen Time. On iPhone, if you choose apps that pull you in, Apple keeps the list and Daybook never learns which apps they are or how long you use them; Apple only tells the app on your phone that a time was passed. The waits, the nudges and the adult-site filter at night (Apple's own) are made on your phone, and none of it is sent, synced, backed up or counted. The number of times you put it down, and which nights the waits held, are kept on your phone only, to show them back to you. You can turn it off in Daybook (Settings, Screen Time) or in the phone's own Settings, under Screen Time, at any time.
  • Anything about the night pack. The automations you make in Shortcuts are yours, kept by Apple on your phone, and Daybook cannot see or change them. Daybook's actions in them read only tonight's start time, and the phone keeps when each one last ran, to show you it is working. None of it is sent, synced or backed up.
  • The trail, for a craving that keeps coming. When it began, the last place you touched, the length you picked, and the times of its notes stay on your phone and are never sent. Its notes are made on your phone too. What you write during its two minutes stays on the screen and is gone when you leave it.
  • Anything from the camera. If you allow it, the trail shows you your own face for a moment, as a live picture. No photo or video is taken, and nothing from the camera is saved or sent.

Apple Health

On an iPhone you can let Daybook read your sleep from Apple Health (Settings, Apple Health, or the hard-days card in Pattern). It is off until you turn it on, and Apple asks you first. Daybook reads only how long you slept each night, for the last 90 nights, and nothing else from Health: no heart rate, no workouts, no other data. It never writes to Health.

Those nights stay on your phone. They are kept apart from your log, so they are never synced to your account, never in a backup, never in what Daybook learns, and never sent anywhere, with or without an account. Daybook uses them only to show you your own pattern: to skip the sleep question in the check-in when Health already knows, to count your Sunday sleep goal, and to compare your nights before days with no slip and before slip days. They are never used for ads, sold or shared. Hours you type yourself in the check-in are part of your log as before, and they win over Health's for that night.

Turning Apple Health off in Daybook (Settings, Apple Health) stops the reading and deletes the nights Daybook kept. Delete everything deletes them too. You can also stop it in the Health app, under Sharing.

Bringing a Mithras journal

If you used Mithras, the app Daybook grew out of, you can bring your Mithras journal and streak into Daybook (Settings, Save a copy, or the link in Mithras). You sign in to Mithras inside Daybook, with Apple or your Mithras email and password. That sign-in goes only to Mithras's own service (Supabase), never to Daybook. Daybook then reads only your own Mithras records, straight from Mithras to your phone: your journal entries (what you wrote, the feelings you picked, and when), the days you marked as wins and setbacks, your longest streak, and the habit and how often you said it happens. It then signs out of Mithras and forgets that sign-in. Your goals, deeper reflections and coach chats stay in Mithras.

What comes over becomes part of your log on this phone, like anything you write in Daybook: it reaches your account only if you have one with sync on. No Daybook server ever sees your Mithras records, and no Daybook account is needed. Bringing it again replaces what came the first time, and never makes a second copy.

What Daybook learns

Daybook is built by one person, who needs to know which parts help people quit and which do not, so they can make it better. So Daybook learns how the app is used, for everyone, from the day it is installed. It is anonymous: it is never tied to your name, your email, your account or your Apple ID, and it never includes your journal, your notes, your voice notes, your plan's words or anything else you type. It is never sold, never shared with anyone, never used for ads, and used only to improve Daybook. Settings, Anonymous data, turns it off, and Delete everything clears what is waiting on your phone.

How it works: every few hours, and as you leave the app, your phone sends a short list of what happened, each as a fixed name with fixed values, the number of days since Daybook was installed on this phone, and the hour of the day (never the minute). The server adds the date the list arrived. The phone keeps a random number for this install, so the list from one phone can be read together and in order (for example, whether an urge passed after a tool, or whether people who finish a lesson slip less the next week). That number is made on your phone, is never part of your log or your account and never sent with them, and a new one is made when you turn this off and on again or use Delete everything. Daybook never joins these lists with accounts, payments or anything else that says who you are. Your internet address is never kept. The lists are stored with Amazon Web Services in the United States, encrypted, readable only by the person who builds Daybook, and deleted after 2 years.

Every name that can be sent, and what it means:

  • open, leave, screen, screen_left, error: Daybook was opened (and whether it started fresh, and whether it was opened by the link from Mithras), left (how long it was open, and from which screen), a screen was opened or left (and how long it was on), or something went wrong (which part).
  • day: once a day, where things stand: the counting mode, the count, no-slip days in the last 28, how many urges were ridden out and how many slips in the last 30 days (two counts, never when or what), level, points, badges, the flame's form, whether it burns low, whether reminders are allowed, whether there is an account, sync or a tip, whether Apple Health is connected (never what it holds), your faith setting, which habit, the phone's language and its system version.
  • setup_page, setup_pick, setup_skip, setup_done: a setup page was reached, a choice picked on it (which one, never typed words), a question skipped, setup finished.
  • lesson_open, lesson_beat, lesson_pick, lesson_done, lesson_left: a lesson (l1 to l49, and the faith lessons g1 to g12) was opened, a part of it reached, an answer picked (which one), finished (and how long it took), or left before the end.
  • sos_open, sos_end, sos_tool, tool_open, tool_done, talk_done: SOS was opened (and from where), ended (how, how long, and how strong the urge was), a tool was tried in SOS (which, and whether the urge passed after it; object is the question “If the urge were a thing”, never which thing was picked), a tool was opened or finished, Talk it through was finished (never what was said).
  • urge, slip, happened, planning, rekindle, comeback: an urge was logged (its strength, which of Daybook's own triggers was picked, a trigger you wrote yourself only as "other", and how it ended), a slip was logged (which trigger, about how long since the one before, and, if a reminder came in the hour before it, that reminder's kind and the hour it came, never the minute), Already happened or Planning a slip was used, a Rekindle step was done, all three flames were lit after a slip.
  • checkin, recheck, sunday, wind: a check-in was saved (the mood, sleep and urge numbers picked), the five questions were answered (the five scores), the Sunday review was finished, Goodnight was said.
  • free: once a month, and once at the end of setup, "How much do you see yourself as someone free of this?" was answered: the number picked from 0 to 10, with how many urges were ridden out and how many slips in the last 30 days, as two counts. Never any words.
  • future_kept, future_heard: a note for the next hard moment was kept, or played or read in SOS (only whether it was note: voice or written; never the note, its words or the recording).
  • chart_seen: a chart was shown (which one, for how long, and whether it was locked).
  • level_up, form, flame_low, flame_lit, badge: a new level, a new flame form, the flame burning low or lit again, a badge earned.
  • nudge, nudge_tap, nudge_did: a reminder of a kind (lesson, checkin, wind, sunday, risk, back, plan) arrived (and the hour it came), Daybook was opened from it (and the hour it came), or what it was for was done that day.
  • ask, ask_tap, bought, account, sync_on, feedback: an ask (for a tip, Daybook Pattern, an account, a rating or a note for the next hard moment) was shown or tapped, a tip or Daybook Pattern was bought (which one, never a card or a receipt), an account was made (with Apple or email, never the account), sync was turned on, feedback was sent (never its words here).
  • import: bringing a Mithras journal over, and how far it got (the link opened, signed in, read, landed, closed or failed), whether the sign-in was Apple or email, and how many entries and Mithras days came, only in bands (none, under 10, under 50, under 200, 200 or more). Never your Mithras account, email or anything you wrote.
  • ab: Daybook tries two wordings of a line to learn which helps more, and this says which one was shown: the line's name (rk_first, Rekindle's first line) and a or b. Which one a phone sees is picked from its random number above, never from anything about you, and is the same every time. With this turned off, you always see the wording Daybook had before.

The values are only numbers, yes or no, or names from Daybook's own lists: cold, secs, route, err, page, ix, lesson, beat, from, outcome, str, tool, chart, locked, trigger, hrs, step, mood, sleep, q1 to q5, free, rode30, slips30, lvl, kind, nh, after, test, arm, what, note, tier, how, mode, count, ns28, pts, badges, low, rem, acct, sync, sup, health, faith, habit, lang, os, stage, ents, days. Anything that is not on those lists is dropped on your phone, and refused by the server.

Never sent: your journal, notes, voice notes, Obsidian folder, plan, reasons, Talk it through, or anything else you write; your name, email, account or Apple ID; what Apple Health holds; the minute anything happened; your location, contacts or the kind of phone.

Anonymous counts

Beside what Daybook learns, the app sends the anonymous counts below, also without asking. They carry no number for your phone: only totals for everyone. Settings, Anonymous data, turns them off with the rest, after which nothing is counted at all, and shows exactly what was sent last.

Once a day your phone sends, for days that are over (and, as you leave the app, once for the day so far), a short list of names and small numbers (each from 1 to 50), plus whether it is an iPhone, Android or the web and the app's version. The server adds them to totals for everyone, per day, and keeps nothing else: not who sent them, not your internet address, not the request. No count can be traced back to you. These are every name that can be sent:

  • act_0, act_1, act_2_6, act_7_13, act_14_29, act_30: Daybook was opened that day, and how many days after it was first opened on this device.
  • ret_7, ret_30: Daybook was opened again 7 or more, or 30 or more, days after it was first opened on this device. Each is sent once.
  • back_1, back_2_3, back_4_7, back_8: Daybook was opened again after a slip, and how many days later.
  • slip_logged, urge_logged: a new slip or urge was logged (only that it was, never when, why or anything written).
  • sos, talk_done, lesson_done, checkin, sunrise, rk_small, rk_phone, goodnight, feedback: SOS was opened, Talk it through finished, a lesson finished, a check-in saved, the sunrise check done, a Rekindle step done, Goodnight said in the wind-down, feedback sent.
  • sos_rode, sos_slip: SOS ended with It passed, or with I slipped.
  • lesson_open: a lesson was opened. ld_ and the lesson's number (l1 to l58; never the faith lessons): which lesson was finished.
  • tab_learn, tab_pattern, tab_more: that tab was opened that day. rem_on: the phone lets Daybook send reminders.
  • nt_ and a kind of reminder (lesson, checkin, wind, sunday, risk, back, plan): a reminder of that kind arrived; with _tap, Daybook was opened from it; with _did, what it was for (the lesson, the check-in, and so on) was done that day.
  • ask_tip, tip_open, acct_open, acct_made: the ask for a tip was shown, the tip page was opened, the account page was opened, an account was made. A tip itself is never counted.
  • tool_ and the tool's name (breathe, ground, stack, curious, pause, thought, feel, forward, move, bed, surprise, halt, message, reasons): that tool was used. With _open after it, the tool was opened.
  • setup_ and a setup page's name (hello, how, habit, freq, when, sos, promise, for, len, faith, creed, tonight, light, account, review), and setup_done: how far setup got, sent once for each page.

Never sent: anything you write, what led to an urge or slip, any time of day, your log, check-ins or plan, your name, email or account.

Feedback

If you send an idea or feedback (Ideas on Today, or Settings), Daybook keeps your words, whether you were on an iPhone, Android or the web, the app's version, and when it arrived. No name and no email are sent, unless you type an address to get a reply. The same words, and that address if you gave one, are emailed to the person who builds Daybook through Amazon's notification service, so they reach a real inbox. Nothing else goes with them: not your log, not your account and not anything about your phone. Feedback is read only by the person who builds Daybook, is deleted from Daybook's store after 180 days, and stays in that inbox until it is deleted there.

Where it is kept, and how it is protected

Account records are stored with Amazon Web Services in the United States. They travel only over encrypted connections and are encrypted where they are stored. Each request is checked against your sign-in, so one person's records can never be sent to another. The server keeps short technical logs (the time of a request and any error, never the content of your records) for 14 days, to keep it running. The counts, what Daybook learns and feedback each have their own store, apart from the records, with technical logs kept for 7 days.

On your phone, you can lock Daybook with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode in Settings, under App lock.

Tips and supporters

Daybook is free. A tip of $5 or more makes you a supporter, which opens sync, a safe copy of your log and the deeper stats. Everything for hard moments stays free for everyone. On iPhone you can tip with Apple's in-app purchase or on the web tip page; on the web, on the web tip page. Apple or Stripe handles the payment, under their own privacy policies (Apple, Stripe). Daybook never sees or keeps your card or bank details.

What Daybook keeps about a tip:

  • Your supporter record: that it is a tip, the amount, the date, and where it came from (Apple, the web tip page, or an account from before tips opened sync). It is kept on your device, in your settings, and, if you have an account, under your account, where it decides whether your account can sync.
  • The payment's reference (Apple's purchase number, or Stripe's checkout number), with the account it opened and when, so the server can check the payment with Apple or Stripe and one payment opens one account. Apple's signed receipt is checked and not kept.
  • When you are signed in, your account ID goes with the tip to Apple or Stripe, so the tip opens your account. Nothing else about you or your log is sent.
  • For a tip on the web tip page, the owner can see in Stripe what Stripe gives a seller: the amount, and the name and email you enter there, used only to keep records and to answer you. Apple gives the owner no name or email.

Delete my account removes your supporter record and the references linked to your account. The supporter record on your device stays after Delete everything, so a tip is never lost; deleting the app removes it.

If you buy Daybook Pattern, Daybook keeps the same kind of record, the same way: the price, the date, when a paid month ends, where it came from, and the payment's reference. It is kept on your device and, if you have an account, under your account, and the rules above apply to it. The monthly read of what changed is worked out on your device from your log, and is never sent anywhere.

Other companies

Amazon Web Services hosts the website and the account service. Apple and Google distribute the app. Apple and Stripe handle tip payments, and Apple handles Sign in with Apple if you use it. If you bring a Mithras journal, your phone signs in to Mithras's service (Supabase) and reads your own records from it, as Mithras itself does. If you have chosen in your phone's settings to share crash reports with app developers, Apple or Google may send Daybook anonymous crash reports. No one else receives anything.

Ratings

At the end of setup, Daybook asks once whether you like it so far. Your answer is kept with your settings, only so it is not asked again, and is never sent as a count. If you say yes, the phone may show Apple's or Google's own rating sheet. A rating you leave there goes to the App Store or Google Play under their privacy policies; Daybook sees only what the store shows publicly.

How long it is kept, and deleting it

  • On your device: until you delete it, or delete the app. On the website, your log is kept by your browser, which may clear it (on an iPhone, after about a week with no visit, unless Daybook is on your Home Screen).
  • In your account: until you delete it.
  • One entry: open the day in Pattern, tap the entry, then Delete. It is removed from your device and your account; the rest of your log and your account stay. The day keeps only a short code that says nothing about the entry, so your other devices do not bring it back. When it was the day's last entry, your account keeps those codes with the deleted day, for the same reason. A journal entry is the same: open it in the journal, then Delete entry.
  • Journal notes: if you turn on Obsidian in Settings to sync your journal with a folder (iPhone only, off until you pick one), each entry is also written as a Markdown file to the folder you pick, on your phone or in iCloud Drive. Daybook reads the notes directly in that folder and no others, on your phone. A note with your first tag comes into your journal as an entry, with one line added to the file to know it by, and is then kept like any other entry, which includes your account if you have one. A note without the tag is never kept or sent anywhere: Daybook remembers only its file name and when it changed, on this phone. The files go nowhere else from Daybook, and the folder's own service, such as iCloud or Obsidian Sync, keeps them by its own rules. Deleting an entry deletes its note. Deleting a note leaves its entry. Stopping the sync, Delete everything and Delete my account leave the notes already there, because they are your files: delete them from the folder.
  • Scripture library (not in the App Store version; only in a test build made for it): it reads the Scriptures folder of the Obsidian vault you pick and writes your verse notes into that vault's Scriptures/Notes folder, on your phone. Nothing from the vault is sent to us. To play a chapter, the phone fetches the recording straight from archive.org, which sees your phone's internet address and which file, like any website. "Open in Gospel Library" opens the chapter in the Church's Gospel Library app or website. Delete everything turns the library off and forgets the vault; your notes stay in the vault, because they are your files.
  • Delete everything (in Settings) clears your log from your device and your account. Your account keeps a copy for 7 days in case it was a mistake or someone else did it, and then it is gone.
  • Delete my account (in Settings, under Sync between devices) removes your sign-in, your records and that copy. The database's own protective backups roll over and are fully gone within 35 days.
  • You can also write to us and we will delete your account.

Your choices

You can see everything Daybook keeps in the app itself, and save your days and entries as a spreadsheet from Settings, under Save a copy. You can change or delete any of it at any time. Wherever you live, if you want a copy of your information, a correction, or a deletion, write to us and it will be done, usually within a few days and always within 30.

Teenagers and children

Daybook is for anyone 16 or older who wants to change a habit, teenagers included. Information from people under 18 is kept and protected exactly as this policy says for everyone: never sold, shared, shown to anyone or used for ads. If you are under 18, a parent or another adult you trust can be a real help, if you choose to tell one.

Daybook does not knowingly collect information from anyone under 16. Without an account it collects nothing that names a person (the anonymous counts described above are never tied to one), and accounts are for people 16 and older. If you believe someone under 16 has made an account, write to us and it will be deleted.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version is posted here with a new date. A change to what is kept or who can see it is also told to you in the app first, and never applied to what you already gave without your yes.
