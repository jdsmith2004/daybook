# How the best apps teach and structure a path: research for Daybook lessons

Researched 2026-11-01 with web search and page reads. Nothing installed, no repository files edited.

How to read this: "Reported" means a number or claim I saw on a page. "Company only" means the company is the only source. "My inference" means my judgment, not a finding. Where a search summary gave a number I could not trace to a page, I left it out or flagged it. Gaps are listed at the end.

---

## The five answers (short)

### 1. Teaching moves worth copying into one lesson a day

1. **One obvious next step, and a unit named for what you will be able to do.** Duolingo went from a tree of skills to one path in 2022 so people stop wondering whether they are using the app "right". Units got descriptive labels such as "get directions" in place of "City 3". (Duolingo blog, below.)
2. **Review lives inside the forward path, not in a separate "practice" place.** Duolingo says "Practice is also forward progress, and now, it's built into your daily lessons!" The science under it is spaced repetition (half-life regression, Birdbrain). For Daybook this means each lesson opens by calling back to an earlier lesson or to the person's own log, in one line, and never as a quiz bank.
3. **Every lesson ends with one small thing the person does or writes in their own life.** The 12 steps are all acts (inventory, list, amends). Lectio 365 ends in "Yield", Glorify in a journal prompt, CBT-i Coach in a sleep diary that turns into a prescription. Plans of the form "if X happens I will do Y" (implementation intentions) have a reported effect of d = 0.65 across 94 tests (Gollwitzer and Sheeran 2006). That is the best-supported "do" step for urges.
4. **Teach with the person's own data.** CBT-i Coach gives a sleep prescription after the diary entries. How We Feel links check-ins to sleep and exercise to show patterns. Sunnyside sends adaptive texts. Baumel and Kane (2018) found "therapeutic persuasiveness" and "therapeutic alliance" each explained 15 to 26 percent of real-world engagement variance across 52 apps and 32 websites. My inference: "this lesson uses what you told me" is the Daybook version of alliance.
5. **A small fixed set of named skills that keep coming back.** How We Feel groups strategies into four themes: Change Your Thinking, Move Your Body, Be Mindful, Reach Out. SMART Recovery has four points (motivation, urges, thoughts and feelings, balance). People can hold four things. They cannot hold forty tips.
6. **Short arcs inside a long path.** Headspace Basics is three courses of 10 sessions. Its 30-day courses are split into three 10-day levels so you can "try the first 10 days and pause". All but one of YouVersion's top 10 completed plans (2015) were a week or less. My inference: chapters of 7 to 10 lessons, each ending in a closing moment.
7. **Treat a slip as a lesson topic, and never let the path punish a missed day.** Self-compassion after a lapse reduced overeating after a diet violation in restrained eaters (Adams and Leary 2007). Reframe users complain the program "just skips ahead" when you miss days. The developer replied that content would pause when users are inactive.
8. **Short "why my brain does this" teaching, always followed by an action.** Reframe's reviewers praise "the short daily articles". Its 160-day reading program is the best-known neuroscience-first daily lesson. The evidence is user praise and company claims only.
9. **A quiet daily bookend, not a game.** I Am Sober's free daily pledge and milestone celebrations; Lectio 365 morning and evening; the Ignatian Examen. Milestones are the most praised feature in I Am Sober reviews. I found no review or study that isolates the pledge's effect.
10. **Let a lesson be skipped or shortened without losing the sequence.** Headspace offers 3, 5 or 10 minute versions of the same course. My inference: Daybook can do this with one "short version" line at the top of a lesson, with no extra menu.

### 2. What makes a path feel real, and a step feel done

A path feels real when four things are true (the first three are visible in Duolingo, Headspace and the 12 steps; the fourth is my inference):

- **It has a shape you can see.** Steps in a fixed order with stages that have names. The 12 steps are numbered and built so each depends on the one before (admit, believe, decide, inventory, admit to others, become ready, ask, list harms, make amends, keep inventory, pray, carry on). Duolingo units name the new thing they add.
- **Each step changes something.** A step is "done" when the person has produced something: a named trigger, a written plan line, a rehearsed response, a decision. A step is only "read" when the exit is "Next".
- **Some steps are earned by events, not by the calendar.** CBT-i Coach waits for sleep diary entries before it gives the prescription (sources disagree on whether it is 5 entries or about 4 weeks of data, so I cannot give the number). My inference: Daybook can release a lesson on "what to do after a slip" the first time the person logs one, or "your hardest hour" once there is a pattern.
- **It ends a chapter on purpose.** Headspace gives a clear finish to the Basics and then offers a choice of next paths. My inference: each Daybook chapter closes with one line that says what the person can now do that they could not 8 days ago, in the person's own words from their log if possible.

Concrete "done" examples:
| App | What the person has after the step |
|---|---|
| 12 steps (AA) | A written inventory, a list of people harmed, an amends made |
| Lectio 365 | A prayer ending in "Yield": a stated surrender for the day |
| Glorify | A journal answer |
| CBT-i Coach | A sleep prescription built from the person's diary |
| Duolingo | A unit finished and a path position that moved; reviews appear ahead of you |
| How We Feel | A named feeling plus a strategy tried |

### 3. How many lessons, and how not to feel like lessons for the sake of lessons

- **No study compares 49, 100 or 365.** I looked and found none. Anything below is my inference from what is reported.
- **Reported lengths:** Reframe 160 days (company); Noom 16 weeks of lessons (reviewers; a 2026 company-funded trial used 16 active weeks then 52 weeks follow-up); Quit Genius 52 weeks with a coach (company-funded trials); CBT-i Coach about 5 weeks (store listing); Headspace Basics 3 x 10 sessions; Woebot's first trial two weeks.
- **Content length and staying length are different problems.** The median 30-day retention across 93 popular mental-health apps is 3.3 percent (Baumel 2019). YouVersion year-long plans lose a third of readers by the end of February and half by May (Bible Gateway's Stephen Smith in Christianity Today, 2015). People leave long paths early, so most people will never see lesson 80 unless lessons 1 to 20 work.
- **My recommendation:** a core path of roughly 50 to 90 lessons in 6 to 9 chapters of 7 to 10, each chapter ending with a threshold moment. 49 (seven chapters of seven) is a fine size if every lesson passes the test below. Going to 100 is worth doing only if each added lesson teaches a different skill. 365 is only safe if it is built as repeating rhythm (a daily reflection) and not as 365 teachings; I found no app that sustains 365 distinct teaching lessons with evidence.
- **Test to keep lessons honest (my inference, drawn from the moves above):** each lesson must (a) name one skill, (b) end with one act, (c) add something the previous lesson did not, and (d) be unmissable-to-skip-ahead: removing it leaves a gap in the path. A lesson that fails (b) or (c) is a candidate to cut or merge.
- **End of path:** Headspace offers choices after the Basics (everyday meditation, courses by length or theme). Duolingo has no end. Noom reportedly follows with a habit-keeping course. Daybook's planned graduation as a permanent award fits; my inference is that after the path the daily lesson becomes a rotating review of the best moves, based on what the person actually used.

### 4. How a faith option is handled well and badly

**Well (reported):**

- The free, short, same-shape daily practice: Pray As You Go (Jesuits in Britain) is free, about 10 to 13 minutes, music plus Scripture plus reflective questions, the same shape every day.
- A named, small, repeatable rhythm: Lectio 365's morning P.R.A.Y. (Pause, Rejoice with a Psalm, Reflect on Scripture, Ask, Yield), midday Lord's Prayer, evening Examen.
- A short devotional with a task: Glorify's reviewers describe Scripture, a short reflection, one or two journal prompts and a prayer in about five minutes (third-party reviews).
- Belief that is open, not required. AA's own steps say "God as we understood Him" (steps 3 and 11) and "a Power greater than ourselves" (step 2). Kelly and colleagues (2011) found AA works in part by increasing spiritual practices, but agnostics and atheists showed the same amount of recovery, so spirituality is one mechanism and not the only one. SMART Recovery says it "neither demands nor demeans" belief.
- A separate full Christian track for people who want one: Celebrate Recovery is Christ-centered, uses eight principles drawn from the Beatitudes plus twelve steps, and names Jesus as the higher power. It works because people choose it; it is not a version of a secular program with verses added.
- Evidence that faith-adapted therapy is acceptable and about as good: a Koenig trial of religiously integrated CBT found improvement similar to conventional CBT, with somewhat more benefit for highly religious patients (small trial, authors say it cannot prove equivalence). Anderson et al. (2015) reviewed 16 studies with significant benefit but flagged researcher and therapist allegiance and other bias.

**Badly (reported, or my inference where marked):**

- A one-line "find your higher power" as the answer to someone with no faith (a forum anecdote, not a survey; I found no survey measuring exclusion).
- Faith that raises shame. Grubbs and colleagues report that perceived addiction to pornography tracks religious or moral disapproval more than the amount of use, and that "moral incongruence" is central to the distress. The APA release says therapists should work "in a non-judgmental way that doesn't induce shame". My inference: faith content must be about grace, help and return, and never about sin counts.
- Being the default for everyone, or being sprinkled as quotes. Only 7 of 170 porn-recovery apps (4.1 percent) had any religion feature (JMIR Formative 2022), so there is little prior art to copy. Hallow and Abide are explicitly Catholic or Christian products; I did not find how any of them handle a person's belief at setup, and the Glorify onboarding was not in any source I found.

**What I would do (my inference):** ask one plain question in setup ("Would you like faith in your lessons?" with yes / a little / no, and "no" is the quiet default), then give every lesson the same core, with one optional short faith paragraph at the end (a Scripture and a one-line prayer). Same lesson, same act, one switch. A person who says "no" never sees a hole. The owner decides how Christian-specific it is; the AA wording ("God as we understood Him") is the widest door and is already familiar to this audience.

### 5. What Daybook should avoid

- **A streak as the center.** 42.4 percent of 170 porn-recovery apps have a streak (JMIR Formative 2022); the authors warn streaks may increase guilt after a relapse. Duolingo evidence on streaks is mixed and mostly qualitative (see the Decision Lab, second-hand). This agrees with the owner's rule that progress is never all or nothing.
- **Gimmick onboarding and paywall tricks.** Quittr: a 40-step onboarding, an early sign-up gate, a 10-question quiz, a "dependency score", a "Sign to Commit" pledge, an 80 percent off one-time offer with a five-minute countdown, a daily message limit on its AI chat, a referral payout (screensdesign teardown). Its most common complaint is cost.
- **Handling sensitive data badly.** 404 Media reported Quittr exposed data on more than 600,000 users, around 100,000 of them self-identified minors, including ages, masturbation frequency and triggers; researchers had warned it for months. (Press reports from a researcher's access; I found no regulator confirmation.) Daybook's privacy rule already covers this, and it is the strongest reason to keep the lessons local.
- **A "likelihood of addiction" score.** BrainBuddy's opening quiz gives a percentage (62 percent for an "occasionally" answer in MEL's test). Moral incongruence research says labeling can itself raise distress.
- **A cutesy or macho tone.** MEL contrasts BrainBuddy's meditating panda with the macho register of NoFap-style apps. Fortify's reviewers praise "calm but direct encouragement" and "progress over perfection".
- **A calendar that outruns the person.** Reframe's skip-ahead complaint. Duolingo's own critics: a linear feed took away choice and agency, and some progress was reset (UX Collective; the author is a critic, not a study).
- **Locking the middle.** Fortify users complained that advanced lessons, community and data were paywalled (2020). Daybook's rule about care never being locked already agrees.
- **A dozen features.** Of 170 porn apps, blocking alone has the most installs but the lowest mean rating (4.04). Diary has the highest mean rating (4.69). Reported by the JMIR authors: no app had instant urge logging with follow-up actions, and none cited efficacy evidence. A narrow, well-built path with logging is the open ground.
- **Quizzes as the way to feel done.** I found no evidence that a quiz makes a step feel finished; the examples above all use an act or a product of the person.
- **Claiming outcomes you cannot support.** Reframe's "91 percent" is company-only. Sunnyside's "33 percent" is a company-promoted analysis with no journal I could find. Noom's trials are company-funded and self-reported. No porn app cited evidence.

## Evidence by app

### Duolingo

- **Why a single path (company):** Duolingo says users were unsure whether they used the app "correctly", and the path gives "a clear path to follow". CEO Luis von Ahn told NBC the aims were "decreasing confusion and increasing learning outcomes". Launched to all users 2022-11-01. Source: https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design ; https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/duolingos-update-redesign-luis-von-ahn-interview-rcna44655
- **Structure:** Old home screen: skills with 5 crown levels plus Legendary. New: each circle is a level, levels from different skills interleaved, grouped into units with descriptive headers, a "guidebook" of tips per unit, Legendary challenges at the end of a unit. Practice, Stories placed along the path. Same blog post.
- **What happened:** the company said internal metrics show "equivalent engagement" with the old design (NBC; company only; no numbers). Backlash was strong (one-star reviews, "worst update ever"). Von Ahn would not reverse it because maintaining two systems was hard. A Q2 2022 shareholder letter excerpt says early results of the redesign were very positive (company only, second-hand excerpt). I did not find a published number isolating the path's effect, and DAU growth later decelerated through 2025 (Q4 2025 letter) without a cause I could attribute to the path.
- **Efficacy claim:** Duolingo's own efficacy page says path learners had higher reading and listening scores than tree learners and scored above the expected A2 level; no numbers in the text, and it is presented as Duolingo research. Source: https://blog.duolingo.com/results-duolingo-efficacy-studies
- **Spaced repetition:** the half-life regression blog reports a mean absolute error of 0.13 on more than 12 million practice sessions (nearly half the Leitner error), and A/B tests that raised daily retention 9.5 percent for practice sessions, 1.7 percent for lessons and 12 percent for overall activity (company). Source: https://blog.duolingo.com/how-we-learn-how-you-learn . The paper: Settles and Meeder 2016, https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/P16-1174.pdf . A 2026 follow-up: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v339/ilidio26a.html
- **Birdbrain:** estimates the learner's ability and the exercise difficulty, to build lessons at the right level; rollout began March 2020 and by October 2020 it personalized over 20 percent of lessons; "more likely to complete more lessons and return day after day", no figures (company). Source: https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain/
- **Critics:** a UX Collective post says the new design removed choice and the Practice Hub tab means it is not really simpler (https://uxdesign.cc/down-the-wrong-path-the-disaster-of-the-latest-duolingo-ui-update-a4cdd1e6ea1c ). On streaks, see https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/consumer-insights/streak-creep-the-perils-of-too-much-gamification (the JCR study is second-hand there; I did not check the original).
- **Checkpoints:** I only found fan-wiki descriptions that disagree with each other, so I do not rely on them.

### Noom

- Daily short articles and quizzes on nutrition, exercise and psychology, CBT-informed; 16 weeks of lessons per reviewers, then a follow-on habit course. Weak on primary sources: affiliate-style reviews. HelpGuide notes going back to a previous lesson is hard and the color-coded foods can cause negative feelings. https://helpguide.org/handbook/healthy-living/noom-review ; https://www.medicspot.co.uk/weight-loss/programmes/noom
- Outcomes: a 2026 Obesity Science & Practice trial of 427 adults (16 weeks active, 52 weeks follow-up) reported 4.1 percent average weight loss against a 1.5 percent gain in a control group given educational materials; funded by Noom, self-reported weight. https://www.santelog.com/actualites-sante-nasdaq/noom-members-kept-losing-weight-full-year-after-program-ended-largest-ever (I read it through a news summary, not the paper). NBC criticized earlier studies for excluding people who stopped using it. https://www.nbcnews.com/think/opinion/weight-loss-app-noom-diet-pandemic-glow-fading-rcna31837
- I could not verify Noom's exact curriculum structure from any primary page.

### Headspace

- Basics: three courses (Basics, Basics 2, Basics 3) of 10 sessions each. Session length options 3, 5 or 10 minutes, then 10 or 15, then 10, 15 or 20. Other courses build on the Basics; you do not have to finish all three first. https://help.headspace.com/hc/en-us/articles/115008275148-How-do-The-Basics-work-and-why-are-they-there
- After the Basics: a daily "Everyday Headspace", courses by length, 30-day courses split into three 10-day levels. https://headspace.com/articles/after-headspace-basics
- Evaluation: a systematic review of 14 RCTs found depression improved in 75 percent of studies that measured it, results for other outcomes mixed, studies underpowered, and half of the RCTs reported a conflict of interest with Headspace or Calm. https://www.epistemonikos.org/en/documents/88e99b5d776b87e99bdbf169dab941f732b12fd8 (via search summary; I did not open the full review). I found no trial isolating the Basics sequence.

### How We Feel

- Free, built with Yale Center for Emotional Intelligence; check-ins on a color-coded emotion grid, tags, HealthKit links to sleep and exercise, weekly insights, strategies of as little as a minute in four themes, share with trusted people. 4.9 stars from about 30K ratings. Data stays on device unless the person opts in to share anonymized data for research. https://apps.apple.com/app/how-we-feel/id1562706384
- The research base is RULER in schools, not the app. I found no efficacy trial of the app. https://medicine.yale.edu/news-article/the-how-we-feel-app-helping-emotions-work-for-us-not-against-us/
- My inference on "clean": one question per screen, a color wall instead of a word list, and detail behind one tap.

### I Am Sober, Reframe, Sunnyside, Quit Genius

- **I Am Sober:** free sobriety counter, daily pledge, community; Sober Plus optional. Most praised: milestones and community. Price is a recurring complaint. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/i-am-sober-sobriety-counter/id672904239 (via search summary; I did not open the page); https://iamsober.com/en/tools/alcohol-app
- **Reframe:** 160-day program of daily reading plus a daily stress and appetite log and a "daily motivation" prompt; courses, toolkit, community, daily Zoom, paid coaching ($99.99 and $249.99 a month in Healthline's review). 4.7 stars from about 45K ratings on iOS. Complaints: cost, extra courses gated behind "coins" earned by daily tasks, and the skip-ahead when you miss days. Evidence: "91 percent" is the company's own claim; I found no peer-reviewed study. https://www.healthline.com/health/mental-health/reframe-app-review ; https://apps.apple.com/app/id1485756576
- **Sunnyside:** third-party analysis of 46,000+ members says about a 33 percent drop in weekly drinks; self-reported, no control, I could not find a journal. https://sunnyside.transistor.fm/episodes/sunnyside-cuts-drinking-by-33-new-study-confirms-w-sunnyside-ceo-nick-allen
- **Quit Genius (Pelago):** digital CBT app plus asynchronous coach; trial of 556 UK smokers versus very brief advice, self-reported abstinence at 26 weeks 27.2 percent versus 16.6 percent; biochemically verified difference at 26 but not 52 weeks; company-funded; effect without the coach not shown. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9597001 (via search summary)

### Porn-specific

- **Landscape (independent):** 170 apps; features: streak 42.4 percent, tutorials 37.1, accountability or community 30, blocking 27.1, rewards 20, religion 4.1; none cited efficacy evidence; no app had real-time urge logging with follow-up; authors warn about streak guilt. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9614626/
- **Fortify:** assessment-guided videos and "Journeys", lessons, coaching, community, check-ins with personalized insights, points and badges; 4.7 from 3.2K ratings; $12 a month, $89.99 a year, $199.99 forever; reviewers praise a non-shaming tone and milestone celebrations; complaints about paywalled content (2020) and bugs. https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fortify-quit-porn-for-good/id1304648824
- **BrainBuddy:** opening quiz with a percentage "likelihood"; logging; meditation-style exercises; a "Life Tree" that grows; community; $12.99 a month; complaints mostly bugs and cost. Source is a MEL article that did not describe lessons. https://melmagazine.com/en-us/story/porn-addiction-apps-brainbuddy-fortify
- **Quittr:** see "What to avoid". https://screensdesign.com/showcase/quittr-quit-porn-now ; https://www.404media.co/viral-quittr-porn-addiction-app-exposed-the-masturbation-habits-of-hundreds-of-thousands-of-users ; https://www.techlicious.com/blog/quittr-porn-addiction-app-leaked-what-users-say-they-watch/
- **Victory (Covenant Eyes):** activity feed, alerts, mini-courses, accountability partner who is sent screenshots; the iOS app is listed as Victory Shield; about 4.3 from about 58,000 ratings; cost and a two-app setup are the main complaints (comparison sites, may be affiliate). https://justuseapp.com/en/app/335318146/covenant-eyes/reviews (not opened)
- **Remojo, Unchained, Reboot Nation:** thin. Remojo has courses, learning paths, meditation, community and accountability and was reportedly built on a model from Paula Hall. Reboot Nation is a community founded 2014 by Gabe Deem, criticized as outdated. I found nothing on Unchained. https://www.insidehook.com/sex-and-dating/men-need-quit-porn-remojo
- **Common complaint across all:** cost, bugs, and paywalling. Praise: not shaming, community, milestones.
- **Gimmicky (my judgment from the above):** Quittr's countdown offers and face-on-panic-button; a "likelihood of addiction" percentage; tree or panda growth without a skill behind it.

### Digital CBT: Woebot, Wysa, and drop-off

- **Baumel 2019 (JMIR 21:e14567, read in full for the abstract and methods):** 93 Android apps with 10,000 or more installs; median daily open rate 4.0 percent; median 15-day and 30-day retention 3.9 and 3.3 percent; trackers (6.1 percent) and peer-support (8.9 percent) and mindfulness (4.7 percent) apps had significantly higher 30-day retention than breathing-exercise apps (0 percent); the authors conclude only a small share of users stay for long. https://jmir.org/2019/9/e14567/PDF
- **Baumel and Kane 2018:** design quality predicts real-world engagement. https://jmir.org/2018/12/e11491/PDF
- **Fleming et al. (cited inside Baumel 2019):** 7 to 42 percent of users of web and app programs engaged moderately; MoodGym had under 1 percent complete all modules (Christensen). Reported inside the Baumel 2019 introduction.
- **Guided vs unguided:** a 2022 Psychological Medicine meta-analysis of 22 studies found guidance raises adherence; a 2021 JMIR Mental Health review reported dropout of 34 percent (guided) and 36 percent (unguided) (https://mental.jmir.org/2021/7/e23091). The two do not agree cleanly; I did not read either in full.
- **Woebot:** Fitzpatrick 2017 RCT, 70 young adults, two weeks, depression (PHQ-9) improved versus an e-book control; anxiety not significant; about 12 engagements per person (company-affiliated authors). https://mental.jmir.org/2017/2/e19/PDF . The consumer app closed on 2025-06-30; the founder cited the cost of FDA marketing authorization, not a failure of the clinical results. https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-mental-health-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/
- **Wysa:** a 2024 JMIR Formative RCT (68 people with arthritis or diabetes) found reduced depression and anxiety, no change in stress; vendor "31 to 40 percent" is a marketing number. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10692879
- **Habit time:** Lally 2010: median 66 days to reach peak automaticity, range 18 to 254 (the high end is extrapolated; the study ran 84 days; the 66 comes from a subset of 39 people; self-reported automaticity of simple behaviors). Secondary sources say one missed day did not materially hurt; I did not confirm that in the paper. https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit

## Gaps and cautions (what I could not verify)

- No primary page for Noom's curriculum structure; no quantitative effect of Duolingo's path in any source I could open; Birdbrain details from the company blog only.
- BrainBuddy, Unchained and Remojo lesson content: nothing solid. Fortify's course content is described only at feature level.
- How Hallow, Glorify and Abide handle a person's belief at setup: not found. The faith-at-setup recommendation above is my own.
- Sunnyside's study and Reframe's outcomes: no peer-reviewed source found.
- The Noom 2026 trial and the Quit Genius numbers I read through summaries, not the papers.
- Several search summaries (CBT-i Coach timing, Karyotaki dropout predictors, Duolingo checkpoints) were conflicting or incomplete, so I left them out of the conclusions.
- Nothing here tests 49 versus 100 versus 365 lessons.
