# God lessons from Latter-day Saint beliefs: what to stand on (November 2, 2026)

The owner is a Latter-day Saint and asked that the God lessons follow those beliefs. A research agent read these pages on November 2, 2026. "Read" means the claim was read on the page itself; "summary" means it came from a search summary; "memory" means it was not checked.

## The Church's recovery program

- Since January 2025 it is "Healing through the Savior: The Addiction Recovery Program", with a 2023 guide (read).
  https://newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org/article/addiction-recovery-program-name-change-emphasizes-healing-begins-with-jesus-christ
- Its twelve key principles, in order (read, 2023 guide; https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/addiction-recovery-program-2023/001-12-steps-of-aa):
  Honesty, Hope, Trust in God, Truth, Confession, Change of Heart, Humility, Prepare to Make Amends, Make Amends, Daily Accountability,
  Personal Revelation, Service. The 2005 guide called 8 and 9 "Seeking Forgiveness" and "Restitution and Reconciliation".
- Meetings are free, confidential, open to people who are not members, in person and online, with a "Pornography Addiction" topic and
  meetings for spouses and family (read). Finder: https://addictionrecovery.churchofjesuschrist.org
- The Church's "Help for Me" page also names Family Services Pornography Addiction Support Groups, Sexaholics Anonymous and Fortify (read).
  https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/help-for-me/understanding-your-pornography-use

## What the Church teaches about pornography that the lessons must stay true to

- Site: https://addressingpornography.churchofjesuschrist.org (Help for Me, for Spouses, for Parents). Five principles: hope and strength in
  Heavenly Father and Jesus Christ; humble, honest, seek truth; connect and build relationships; make and adjust plans; be accountable (read).
- Levels of involvement, from President Oaks: inadvertent exposure (no sin if you turn away), occasional use, intensive or habitual use,
  compulsive use (addiction). Calling oneself addicted "can be harmful and misleading if it isn't true". "Change is always possible." Even
  occasional use is called harmful (read, understanding-your-pornography-use).
- Guilt ("I have done a bad thing") is kept, shame ("I am a bad person") is avoided; people are not labeled by their behavior; the focus is
  their divine identity (read, leaders' page: https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/counseling-resources/pornography).
- "Rather than measuring our success by sequential days of abstinence, we can measure success by how consistently we turn to Christ"
  (read, https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/help-for-me/what-should-i-focus-on/principle-5). Principle 2: most people slip,
  change is a process, secrecy leads to shame (read).
- God's love is endless, and no one is beyond the help of Jesus Christ and His Atonement (read,
  https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/help-for-me/does-god-still-love-me).
- The bishop: confession to the bishop is part of repentance for serious sins. For pornography the General Handbook says personal counseling
  and informal restrictions are usually enough; a council is for intensive, compulsive use that has harmed a marriage or family, and is
  required for child pornography (read, https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/general-handbook/32-repentance-and-membership-councils).
  Step 5 of the program includes the bishop. So: encouraged, and part of repentance for serious or habitual use. "Required" overstates it.

These line up with Daybook's own rules: progress is not a count of days in a row, a slip is not who a person is, and "addict" is not a label
to put on someone who rarely watches.

## Quoting and the Church's name (not legal advice)

- The Church's site is for personal, noncommercial use; use by an organization needs written permission, asked through
  permissions.ChurchofJesusChrist.org, about 45 days (read, https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/legal/terms-of-use). So the program guide,
  conference talks and the Church's pages are paraphrased in Daybook's own words and named, not quoted, unless permission is given.
- The King James Bible is free to quote. The early texts of the Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants and Pearl of Great Price are generally
  thought public domain in the US; the 2013 edition's headings, footnotes and study helps belong to the Church (summary and memory, low
  confidence). Plan: short verses only, never headings or footnotes, and a lawyer's read or the Church's permission before the God lessons ship.
- The Church asks that "Mormon" and "LDS" not be used as its name (read, https://newsroom.churchofjesuschrist.org/style-guide). Daybook says
  "Latter-day Saint" and "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints", uses no logo, and says plainly it is not made by the Church.

## Research

- Grubbs and others 2015: religiosity predicts feeling addicted through moral disapproval, even with actual use held equal; among users,
  religiosity does not predict how much they watch. Moderate. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24519108/
- Grubbs, Perry and others 2019, the moral incongruence model and meta-analysis. Moderate to strong; a later preregistered study found only
  weak support for religiosity itself predicting perceived addiction (summary). https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30076491/
- Willoughby, Leonhardt and Young-Petersen 2017, "Damaged Goods", 686 unmarried adults: feeling addicted partly carries the link from
  religiosity to worry about relationships. Moderate, cross-sectional. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28287845/
- Sorrell and others 2024, with a Latter-day Saint sample: people who attend worship often see their viewing as compulsive at lower amounts.
  Moderate; the closest finding on Latter-day Saints. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38741573/
- 2021 preregistered study, 645 theistic users: religiosity leads to shame, which leads to feeling addicted, mainly with high scrupulosity or
  moral disapproval. Moderate. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33403534/
- Crosby and Twohig 2016: acceptance and commitment therapy against a waitlist, 28 men, all but one Latter-day Saints. Viewing fell 93 percent
  against 21; 35 percent had stopped at three months. A small randomized trial, self-report. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27157029/
  It is the strongest trial on porn there is, and it was done with the people these lessons are written for.
